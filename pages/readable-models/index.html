Why a readable
formula matters.

The ambition behind Alvyn Symbol: make the relationship part of the conversation.

A prediction answers one question: what might happen? An explicit mathematical model invites another: how do these quantities relate?

A useful starting point

Consider an illustrative model, y = ax² + bx + c. Its structure is visible. You can ask what happens when x increases, inspect the coefficients, and consider whether the shape makes sense for the problem.

This is the appeal of symbolic modelling: a relationship expressed in mathematical terms that people can inspect and discuss.

Readable does not mean proven

A simple equation can still be wrong. A close fit to observed data does not establish causation, and predictions outside the observed range can be unreliable. Domain knowledge and evaluation on unseen observations remain essential.

What belongs on the page?

The formula is only the beginning. A useful one-page explanation also records the variables and units, the data used, the evaluation method, and the conditions under which the model should be used.

The Alvyn direction

Alvyn Symbol brings this ambition to an approachable workflow. The aim is to help people move from observations to expressions they can question, explain, and build on.
